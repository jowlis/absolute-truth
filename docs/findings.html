 The CHSH self-testing theorem establishes that maximal violation of the CHSH inequality (⟨C⟩ = 2√2) uniquely certifies, up to local isometries, the presence of a maximally entangled two-qubit state |Φ⁺⟩ = (1/√2)(|00⟩+|11⟩) and anti-commuting measurement observables. Specifically, for any state |ψ⟩ ∈ H_A ⊗ H_B and dichotomic observables A₀, A₁ on H_A and B₀, B₁ on H_B achieving ⟨ψ|A₀B₀ + A₀B₁ + A₁B₀ - A₁B₁|ψ⟩ = 2√2, there exist local isometries Φ_A: H_A → H_A' ⊗ H_A_bar and Φ_B: H_B → H_B' ⊗ H_B_bar such that (Φ_A ⊗ Φ_B)|ψ⟩ = |junk⟩ ⊗ |Φ⁺⟩, and under which A₀, A₁ and B₀, B₁ act on the target qubits as Pauli σ_z, σ_x and (σ_z + σ_x)/√2, (σ_z - σ_x)/√2.

 The Mayers-Yao self-testing protocol (Mayers & Yao, 2004) device-independently certifies a maximally entangled state |Φ⁺⟩ and reference measurement choices using 3 measurement settings per party (x,y ∈ {0,1,2}). In robust self-testing, if observed correlation statistics P(ab|xy) deviate by ε in trace distance or L₁ norm from ideal target statistics, the physical state |ψ⟩ satisfies ||(Φ_A ⊗ Φ_B)|ψ⟩ - |junk⟩ ⊗ |Φ⁺⟩||₂ = O(√ε) (with O(ε) achieved under optimized robust self-testing bounds), guaranteeing quantitative state certification under experimental noise.

 Parallel self-testing protocols establish device-independent certification of N-copy tensor product states |Φ⁺⟩^{⊗N} and high-dimensional quantum systems (C^{2^N} ⊗ C^{2^N}) using parallelized Bell tests. Specifically, testing N maximally entangled qubit pairs simultaneously under parallel CHSH inequalities certifies that the shared state is locally isometric to |junk⟩ ⊗ (|Φ⁺⟩)^{⊗N} with a number of measurement choices per party that remains fixed at 2 (independent of N) or scales polynomial in N, enabling efficient high-dimensional quantum hardware verification.

 Self-testing rigidity characterizes the boundary ∂Q of the quantum correlation set: a correlation point P* ∈ ∂Q is rigid if and only if all physical quantum realizations (H_A, H_B, |ψ⟩, {A_a^x}, {B_b^y}) yielding P* are unique up to a local isometry Φ = Φ_A ⊗ Φ_B. Exposed vector extremal points of Q that maximize linear Bell inequalities (e.g., CHSH, tilted CHSH, and CGLMP inequalities) are rigid self-tests, and every pure bipartite entangled state can be self-tested by maximal violation of an appropriate Bell inequality.
