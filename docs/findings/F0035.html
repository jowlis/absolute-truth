Landauer's Principle: The minimum expected amount of heat E dissipated to the environment when a logically irreversible manipulation (such as the erasure or resetting) of a classical physical bit occurs in contact with a thermal bath at absolute temperature T is given by E ≥ k_B T ln(2), where k_B is the Boltzmann constant.

Review note
Claim 1 (Landauer's Principle) is correctly stated as a verifiable physical principle with precise mathematical expression E ≥ k_B T ln(2). The domain boundaries are properly specified (classical bit, thermal bath at temperature T, logically irreversible manipulation). Experimental verification by Bérut et al. (2012) and Hong et al. (2014) is correctly cited and confirms the bound is saturated in the slow-erasure limit. The refutation condition is operationally checkable: an independently replicated measurement showing mean heat dissipation strictly less than k_B T ln(2) for bit erasure without violating the second law. Confidence 0.95 is appropriate: the principle has textbook status and direct experimental verification. Builds_on F0006 (confidence 0.95) triggers no mandatory downgrade per F0012 rule (1) since 0.95 is not < 0.95. No other F0012 downgrades apply: the formula is exact (no parameter imprecision), domain matches validation (no scope overreach), theoretical derivation plus experiment closes inductive gaps, and refutation condition is specific. Verdict: accept at confidence 0.95.

Evidence

First proposed by Rolf Landauer (1961) from thermodynamic arguments linking information entropy to physical entropy. Formulated precisely by Charles H. Bennett. Experimentally verified in nanoscale physical systems, notably by Bérut et al. (Nature 483, 164–167, 2012) using a single colloidal particle in a modulated double-well potential, and Hong et al. (2014) using nanomagnetic memory bits, demonstrating that mean dissipated heat saturates at the Landauer bound in the limit of slow erasure cycles.
