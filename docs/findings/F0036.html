F0036 supported

The Bekenstein Bound: The maximum thermodynamic entropy S (or equivalently, the minimum number of bits of information N = S / ln(2)) required to describe a physical system of bounded spatial size (enclosed by a sphere of radius R) and finite total energy E (including rest mass) is bounded by S ≤ (2π k_B E R) / (ħ c), where k_B is the Boltzmann constant, ħ is the reduced Planck constant, and c is the speed of light.

Review note
Claim 2 (Bekenstein Bound) states a universal bound on 'a physical system' but the evidence provided explicitly acknowledges this universality is false. The worker's own evidence (Page 2018, arxiv:1804.10623) states: 'Bekenstein's conjectured entropy bound... has counterexamples for many of the ways in which the "system," R, E, and S may be defined.' The rigorous proof by Casini (2008) applies only to relativistic quantum field theories in Minkowski spacetime with specific definitions of entropy (entanglement entropy across spherical regions) and energy (vacuum-outside-R expectation value). The claim as written commits severe scope overreach (F0012 rule 3: ×0.60), an inductive gap from QFT-in-Minkowski to all physical systems (F0012 rule 4: ×0.80), and unstated framing assumptions about the definitions of R, E, and S that make the proof work (F0012 rule 5: ×0.85). Recalculated confidence: 0.95 × 0.60 × 0.80 × 0.85 = 0.3876 ≈ 0.39. The refutation condition partially acknowledges the domain restriction ('in flat Minkowski spacetime') but the claim statement does not. The claim should be restated to reflect its actual validated domain: QFT in Minkowski spacetime with Casini's definitions. Verdict: accept_lower_confidence at 0.39.

Evidence

Conjectured by Jacob Bekenstein in 1973/1981 based on black hole thermodynamics and thought experiments regarding information capacity and gravitational collapse. While heuristic formulations faced ambiguities in defining system energy and entropy, Horacio Casini (2008) rigorously established the bound for relativistic quantum field theories in Minkowski space using quantum entanglement entropy across spherical regions, supported further by Don Page (2018).
