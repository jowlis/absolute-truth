F0080 supported

Domain boundaries and complementary utility of the Margolus-Levitin theorem require: (1) A quantum system governed by a time-independent Hamiltonian H bounded from below (with ground state energy convention E_0 = 0); (2) Finite mean excitation energy \langle H \rangle < \infty; (3) The bound is tightest when the energy dispersion \Delta E is large compared to \langle H \rangle (e.g., for multi-level superposition states with high-frequency components), whereas the Mandelstam-Tamm bound is tightest when \Delta E is small compared to \langle H \rangle (e.g., for narrow two-level systems). Combining both yields the unified quantum speed limit \tau \geq \max \left( \frac{\pi \hbar}{2 \Delta E}, \frac{\pi \hbar}{2 \langle H \rangle} \right).

Review note
Complementary utility and unified bound are accurately described: MT tightest when ΔE ≪ ⟨H⟩ (e.g., two-level systems), ML tightest when ΔE ≫ ⟨H⟩ (e.g., multi-level superpositions). The unified bound τ ≥ max(πħ/(2ΔE), πħ/(2⟨H⟩)) is mathematically correct. Domain conditions (time-independent H, bounded below, E₀=0, finite ⟨H⟩) are properly stated. Citations to Giovannetti et al. (2003) and Deffner & Lutz (2017) are authoritative. Confidence 0.97 is well-calibrated.

Evidence

Comparative analysis of MT and ML bounds by Giovannetti, Lloyd, and Maccone (2003, Phys. Rev. A 67, 052109) and Deffner & Lutz (2017). The two bounds probe complementary aspects of quantum dynamics: Mandelstam-Tamm depends on energy variance (fluctuations), while Margolus-Levitin depends on mean energy (total excitation).

Sources

  • V. Giovannetti, S. Lloyd, and L. Maccone, 'Quantum limits to dynamical evolution', Phys. Rev. A 67, 052109 (2003).
  • S. Deffner and S. Lutz, 'Quantum Speed Limits: The First Sixty Years', J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 50, 335302 (2017).
