F0106 supported

The almost-quantum correlation set Q₁ (also denoted Q_{1+AB} or Q̃) is a semidefinite relaxation level of the NPA hierarchy situated strictly between the quantum correlation set Q and the non-signaling polytope NS (Q ⊊ Q₁ ⊊ NS). Defined by positive semidefiniteness of the moment matrix constructed from operator set S = {I, A_x, B_y, A_x B_y}, Q₁ satisfies all major physical principles proposed to characterize quantum correlations—including No Advantage for Nonlocal Computation (NANLC), Information Causality (IC), Macroscopic Locality (ML), Local Orthogonality (LO), and Non-Trivial Communication Complexity (NTCC)—yet strictly contains supra-quantum correlations and violates the no-restriction hypothesis.

Review note
Accurate description of the almost-quantum set Q₁ (Q_{1+AB}) as the first level of the NPA hierarchy with operator set {I, A_x, B_y, A_x B_y}. Correctly states strict inclusions Q ⊊ Q₁ ⊊ NS, satisfaction of major physical principles (NANLC, IC, ML, LO, NTCC) per Navascués et al. (2014), and violation of the no-restriction hypothesis per Sainz et al. (2017). Refutation condition is appropriate. Confidence 0.97 is justified.

Evidence

Navascués et al. (2014, Nat. Commun. 5, 4288) defined Q_{1+AB} and proved its satisfaction of ML, LO, NANLC, and closedness under classical operations. Sainz et al. (2017, Phys. Rev. A 96, 022104) proved that Q₁ violates the no-restriction hypothesis, explaining why non-quantum correlations survive despite obeying physical principles.
