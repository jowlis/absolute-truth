Review note
The Church-Turing thesis is widely recognized in logic and philosophy of computation as a conceptual thesis equating an informal notion ('effective calculability') with a formal model (Turing computability). It is not a mathematical theorem and cannot be proved purely deductively. The statement, type (conceptual), confidence, and refutation condition are all appropriate. The evidence cites standard references (SEP, Kleene). Accept at stated confidence.

Evidence

Logical literature (Church, Kleene, Turing, Gödel) recognizes that equating an informal, intuitive notion of human/mechanical computation with a formal mathematical model cannot be proved purely mathematically.

Sources

  • https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/church-turing/
  • Kleene, S. C. (1952). Introduction to Metamathematics. D. Van Nostrand.
