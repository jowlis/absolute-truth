F0022 supported

Matiyasevich's Theorem (MRDP Theorem / Hilbert's 10th Problem): Every computably enumerable set of natural numbers is Diophantine; consequently, there exists no general algorithm to determine whether an arbitrary multivariable polynomial equation with integer coefficients p(x_1, ..., x_n) = 0 has solutions in integers (or non-negative integers). Domain boundaries: Applies to multivariable integer polynomials over Z or N; does not apply to linear or single-variable Diophantine equations, two-variable quadratic equations, or polynomial equations over real numbers R (decidable by Tarski's quantifier elimination) or complex numbers C (decidable via Gröbner bases).

Evidence

Proved by Yuri Matiyasevich (1970), completing the work of Martin Davis, Hilary Putnam, and Julia Robinson (1961). The MRDP theorem establishes that bounded existential quantifiers and exponential relations can be represented by pure Diophantine equations, allowing Turing machine execution to be encoded as Diophantine equations. Mechanically verified in Coq (Larchey-Wendling & Forster, 2019).
