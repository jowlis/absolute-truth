CMP-005: The question 'Does Cox's theorem uniquely justify Bayesian probability as the only consistent extension of Boolean logic to degrees of belief, given its postulates (divisibility, comparability, associativity of conjunction, and continuity)?' is classified as 'purely-conceptual' (type: conceptual). Cox's theorem (1946, 1961) derives the sum and product rules of probability from postulates about plausible reasoning. However, the theorem's assumptions (particularly differentiability/continuity and the functional equation form) have been debated (Paris 1994; Van Horn 2003). Alternative foundations (de Finetti's Dutch book, Savage's axioms, Jaynes' desirata) exist. The question is purely-conceptual because it concerns the interpretation and justification of a mathematical framework — there is no empirical test that could distinguish 'Bayesian probability as uniquely justified' from 'Bayesian probability as one coherent framework among others' when all yield identical numerical calculations.
