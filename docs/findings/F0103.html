F0103 supported

The Kochen-Specker theorem (Kochen & Specker, 1967; Bell, 1966) proves that for any Hilbert space of dimension d ≥ 3, there is no non-contextual hidden-variable theory that assigns definite values (0 or 1) to all projection operators (or equivalently, to all yes-no observables) such that: (i) the value assigned to the identity operator is 1; (ii) for any set of mutually orthogonal projections summing to identity, exactly one is assigned value 1. This establishes quantum contextuality: measurement outcomes cannot be pre-determined independently of the measurement context (which compatible observables are measured jointly).

Review note
The claim provides an exact mathematical statement of the Kochen-Specker theorem for Hilbert space dimension d ≥ 3, defining non-contextual value assignments on projection operators and citing Kochen & Specker (1967) and Bell (1966).

Evidence

Kochen & Specker, 'The Problem of Hidden Variables in Quantum Mechanics', J. Math. Mech. 17, 59 (1967). The original proof uses 117 vectors in ℝ³. Simpler proofs exist: Peres (1991) uses 33 rays, Conway & Kochen (31), and the minimal known is 18 vectors (Arends et al. 2011, lower bound 18). The theorem is equivalent to the statement that the orthogonality graph of quantum projectors in d ≥ 3 has chromatic number > d (no consistent 0/1 coloring). Gleason's theorem (1957) is a related continuous analogue.
