F0016 supported

Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem: Any consistent, recursively axiomatized formal theory T capable of interpreting Robinson arithmetic Q (or Peano arithmetic PA) and satisfying the Hilbert-Bernays-Löb provability conditions cannot prove the sentence Con(T) representing its own consistency (i.e., T ⊬ Con(T)). Domain boundaries: Requires recursive axiomatizability, consistency, and sufficient strength to formalize provability and syntax; does not apply to complete decidable theories (e.g., Presburger arithmetic, Tarski's real closed fields), inconsistent theories (which prove everything), or non-effective theories (e.g., true arithmetic Th(N)).

Gödel's Second Incompleteness Theorem is stated precisely with correct hypotheses (consistent, recursively axiomatized, interprets Q/PA, Hilbert-Bernays-Löb conditions). Domain boundaries correctly exclude decidable theories (Presburger, real closed fields), inconsistent theories, and non-effective theories. Refutation condition is operationally specific. Type 'verifiable' is correct. Builds_on dependencies (F0001, F0009) are appropriate.

Evidence

First proved by Kurt Gödel (1931) and formalized via Hilbert-Bernays-Löb derivability conditions. The proof demonstrates via diagonal construction that T ⊢ Con(T) implies T ⊢ G (the Gödel sentence), which contradicts consistency. Mechanically verified in Isabelle/HOL (L. Paulson, 2014) and Coq (R. O'Connor, 2005). Textbook consensus acceptance across mathematical logic.
