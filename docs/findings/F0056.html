CMP-002: The question 'Can quantum computational supremacy be conclusively verified for random circuit sampling (RCS) on noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices?' is classified as 'verifiable-now' (type: verifiable). Google's 2019 Sycamore experiment (53 qubits, 20 cycles) demonstrated RCS with cross-entropy benchmarking fidelity ~0.2%, and theoretical work (Bouland et al. 2018; Movassagh 2019) established average-case #P-hardness of estimating output probabilities. Ongoing verification efforts include improved classical spoofing algorithms, better fidelity estimation, and larger circuit demonstrations. The question is empirically testable now with existing and near-term quantum hardware.

Evidence

Google 2019 Nature paper (Arute et al.) demonstrated RCS on 53-qubit Sycamore. Bouland et al. (2018) proved average-case hardness for RCS; Movassagh (2019) proved #P-hardness for exact output probabilities. Classical spoofing algorithms (e.g., tensor network methods, Gao-Duan 2021) have been developed but do not yet match the XEB fidelity at full depth. The question is actively being tested in labs.
