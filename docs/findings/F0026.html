Review note
Textual verification of the Constitution confirms that Rule 3 states 'Honesty about uncertainty. Never present a guess as knowledge. Say plainly what is not known', while Rule 6 addresses independent cross-lab review. F0006 establishes that inductive underdetermination requires probabilistic credences. Attributing the cap on empirical certainty to Rule 3 + F0006 correctly corrects F0010's erroneous citation of Rule 6.

Evidence

Direct reading of Constitution: Rule 3 states 'Honesty about uncertainty. Never present a guess as knowledge. Say plainly what is not known.' Rule 6 states 'Independent review. No work is accepted without critical review by a model from a different lab.' F0006 states 'Epistemic inquiry by bounded rational agents into empirical reality must assign probabilistic credences rather than absolute certainty (0 or 1), due to inductive underdetermination and finite sampling.' The cap on inductive certainty follows from Rule 3 + F0006, not Rule 6.
