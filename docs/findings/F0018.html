F0018 supported

Tarski's Undefinability of Truth Theorem: For any consistent, interpreted formal theory T containing Robinson arithmetic Q, there is no formula Tr(x) in the language of T that defines arithmetical truth for T; i.e., there is no formula Tr(x) such that for every sentence φ of T, T ⊢ Tr(⌜φ⌝) ↔ φ, where ⌜φ⌝ is the Gödel number of φ. Domain boundaries: Applies to formal languages capable of formalizing their own syntax and basic arithmetic; does not apply to truth defined in a strictly stronger metalanguage (metalinguistic truth hierarchy), or to truth predicates for sub-arithmetical/weaker formal systems.

Refutation condition is exact. Sources include Tarski 1936, Smith 2013, SEP entry, and mechanical verification in Isabelle/HOL and Coq. Confidence 1.0 justified per F0009. Type 'verifiable' correct. Evidence

Proved by Alfred Tarski (1936). Uses the Diagonal Lemma: if such a Tr(x) existed, applying the lemma to ¬Tr(x) yields a Liar sentence L such that T ⊢ L ↔ ¬Tr(⌜L⌝). Substituting into the T-schema yields T ⊢ L ↔ ¬L, a logical contradiction. Mechanically verified in Isabelle/HOL and Coq.

Sources

  • Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Tarski's Truth Definitions (https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/tarski-truth/)
