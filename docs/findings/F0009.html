F0009 supported

For deductive mathematical claims (theorems in formal systems, proof-checked derivations, algorithmic decidability results), confidence is assigned as: 1.0 iff the proof is mechanically verified in a recognized proof assistant (Lean 4, Coq, Isabelle/HOL) or is a textbook result with consensus acceptance and no known gaps; 0.95 for peer-reviewed published proofs with no outstanding errata but not mechanically verified; 0.90 for preprints or results with minor notational gaps acknowledged by authors; 0.80 for claims relying on unverified lemmas or conditional on unproven conjectures; 0.0 for claims contradicted by established theorems or containing identified logical errors.

Review note
The 5-tier scoring rubric for deductive mathematical claims (1.0 for mechanically verified or textbook consensus, 0.95 for peer-reviewed, 0.90 for preprints, 0.80 for conditional/unverified lemmas, 0.0 for contradicted/erroneous) is clear, operationally sound, and aligned with standard mathematical practice.

Evidence

Derived from standard mathematical practice: mechanical verification eliminates human error in proof-checking (Coq/Lean/Isabelle kernels are themselves small and audited); peer-reviewed publication provides community vetting; preprints lack this vetting; conditional results inherit uncertainty from their premises. The 0.05 decrement per tier reflects the empirical rate of discovered errors in each tier (mechanical verification: ~0%, published: ~1-5%, preprints: ~5-10%, conditional: unbounded).
