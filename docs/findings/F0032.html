First-order Presburger Arithmetic (the theory Th(N, +, 0, 1, =, <) without multiplication) is an algorithmically decidable theory: there exists a total decision procedure (such as Cooper's algorithm or quantifier elimination) that decides the truth of any sentence in L_Presburger, as mechanically verified in Isabelle/HOL, Coq, and Lean 4.

Evidence

Originally proven decidable by Mojżesz Presburger (1929) via quantifier elimination. Mechanically verified decision procedures exist in Isabelle/HOL (HOL/Decision_Procedures/Presburger.thy implemented by Chaieb & Nipkow), Coq (Coq.micromega.Lia / ZCooper implementing Cooper's algorithm for linear integer arithmetic), and Lean 4 (Mathlib.Tactic.Omega / core omega decision tactic). Contrasts with first-order logic undecidability (F0017) and Peano arithmetic incompleteness (F0001).
