F0046 supported

The speed of light in vacuum c is defined to be exactly 299,792,458 m/s by the international definition of the metre (SI, since 1983; reaffirmed in 2019), meaning that dimensional 'variation of c' is not experimentally meaningful in SI units; any substantive physical variation must manifest in dimensionless combinations such as the fine-structure constant α or relative standards. Lorentz-invariance and Standard-Model Extension (SME) tests constrain SME photon/electron coefficients at the 10^-18 to 10^-19 level, and gamma-ray dispersion constraints approach the Planck scale, testing deviations from Lorentz symmetry rather than a varying c in SI.
