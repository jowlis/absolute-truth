F0021 supported

Post's Correspondence Problem (PCP) Undecidability: The decision problem of determining whether, for an arbitrary finite collection of pairs of words (u_i, v_i) over an alphabet Σ with |Σ| ≥ 2, there exists a sequence of indices (i_1, i_2, ..., i_k) such that u_{i_1}...u_{i_k} = v_{i_1}...v_{i_k}, is algorithmically undecidable. Domain boundaries: Applies to general PCP over alphabets of size |Σ| ≥ 2 and arbitrary pair counts; does not apply to unary alphabets (|Σ| = 1, which is decidable via linear arithmetic / Presburger arithmetic), small pair counts (n ≤ 2), or bounded-length matching sequences.

Evidence

Proved by Emil Post (1946). Undecidability is established by constructing a reduction from Turing machine acceptance, encoding valid computation histories as matching tile strings of PCP. Mechanically verified in Coq (Larchey-Wendling & Forster, 2019). Textbook consensus.
