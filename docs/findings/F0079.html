F0079 supported

The Margolus-Levitin (ML) theorem bounds the minimum evolution time \tau for a quantum system to evolve from an initial state \psi(0) to an orthogonal state \psi(\tau) \langle \psi(0)|\psi(\tau) \rangle = 0 under a time-independent Hamiltonian H with ground state energy set to zero (E_0 = 0), given by \tau \geq \frac{\pi \hbar}{2 \langle H \rangle}, where \langle H \rangle = \langle \psi(0)| H |\psi(0) \rangle$ is the mean excitation energy above the ground state.

Review note
The Margolus-Levitin theorem is correctly formulated: τ ≥ πħ/(2⟨H⟩) with ground state energy E₀ = 0, for orthogonal state evolution under time-independent Hamiltonian. The mean excitation energy ⟨H⟩ is properly defined. Citations to Margolus & Levitin (1998, Physica D) and Giovannetti, Lloyd & Maccone (2003, PRA) are exact and verifiable. Confidence 0.98 is appropriate for this rigorously proven theorem. Type 'verifiable' is correct.

Evidence

Formulated by Norman Margolus and Lev B. Levitin (1998, Physica D 120, 188). Proven by expanding the time-evolved state \psi(t) = \sum_n c_n e^{-i E_n t / \hbar} \phi_n in energy eigenstates \phi_n with E_0 = 0, and showing that the real part of the inner product \langle \psi(0)|\psi(t) \rangle$ must vanish at least as fast as cos(\langle H \rangle t / \hbar)$ when energy is positive.

Sources

  • N. Margolus and L. B. Levitin, 'The maximum speed of dynamical evolution', Physica D 120, 188 (1998).
  • V. Giovannetti, S. Lloyd, and L. Maccone, 'Quantum limits to dynamical evolution', Phys. Rev. A 67, 052109 (2003).
