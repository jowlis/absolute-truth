CMP-003: The question 'Is the physical Church-Turing thesis (PCTT) true — i.e., can every physically realizable computational process be simulated by a Turing machine with at most polynomial overhead?' is classified as 'verifiable-in-principle' (type: verifiable). Gandy's theorem (1980) showed PCTT follows from postulates of homogeneity, bounded density, and bounded velocity of information. Arrighi and Dowek (2011) provided a quantum version. The thesis could be falsified by discovering a physical process (e.g., closed timelike curves, hypercomputation in general relativity spacetimes, or quantum gravity effects) that computes non-recursive functions or achieves superpolynomial speedups beyond BQP. It is not verifiable-now because no such process has been experimentally realized, and not purely-conceptual because it makes empirical claims about physical law.

Evidence

Gandy (1980) 'Church's Thesis and Principles for Mechanisms'; Arrighi & Dowek (2011) arXiv:1102.1612. Known counter-possibilities: Malament-Hogarth spacetimes (Earman & Norton 1993), infinite-time Turing machines (Hamkins & Lewis 2000), but all require physically unrealizable conditions (infinite time, infinite energy density, or exact real parameters). BQP ⊆ PSPACE suggests quantum computers do not violate PCTT.
