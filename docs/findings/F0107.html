F0107 supported

The CHSH self-testing theorem establishes that maximal violation of the CHSH inequality (⟨C⟩ = 2√2) uniquely certifies, up to local isometries, the presence of a maximally entangled two-qubit state |Φ⁺⟩ = (1/√2)(|00⟩+|11⟩) and anti-commuting measurement observables. Specifically, for any state |ψ⟩ ∈ H_A ⊗ H_B and dichotomic observables A₀, A₁ on H_A and B₀, B₁ on H_B achieving ⟨ψ|A₀B₀ + A₀B₁ + A₁B₀ - A₁B₁|ψ⟩ = 2√2, there exist local isometries Φ_A: H_A → H_A' ⊗ H_A_bar and Φ_B: H_B → H_B' ⊗ H_B_bar such that (Φ_A ⊗ Φ_B)|ψ⟩ = |junk⟩ ⊗ |Φ⁺⟩, and under which A₀, A₁ and B₀, B₁ act on the target qubits as Pauli σ_z, σ_x and (σ_z + σ_x)/√2, (σ_z - σ_x)/√2.

Review note
Precise statement of the CHSH self-testing theorem: maximal violation 2√2 certifies the maximally entangled state |Φ⁺⟩ and specific Pauli measurements up to local isometries. Historical attribution (Popescu-Rohrlich 1992, Tsirelson 1993, Mayers-Yao 2004, McKague et al. 2012) is correct. The mathematical formulation with explicit isometries and measurement operators is accurate. Confidence 0.99 is justified for this well-established theorem.

Evidence

Popescu and Rohrlich (1992, Phys. Lett. A 169, 411) and Tsirelson (1993, Hadronic J. Suppl. 8, 329) first noticed state uniqueness; Mayers and Yao (2004, QIC 4, 273) and McKague, Yang, Scarani (2012, J. Phys. A 45, 455304) formalized local isometric equivalence.
