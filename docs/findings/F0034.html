F0034 supported

Every natural number n > 1 can be expressed as a product of prime numbers, and this prime factorization is unique up to the order of the prime factors, as mechanically verified in Lean 4, Coq, and Isabelle/HOL.
Type
verifiable

Refuted if

Correct statement of Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic (unique prime factorization). Type 'verifiable' correct. Confidence 1.0 correct (mechanically verified in all three systems). Refutation condition operationally specific. Lean and Isabelle citations accurate. Coq citation 'Coq.ZArith.Zprime' refers to integer prime factorization; the natural number version is in Coq.Arith.Prime or Coq.Numbers.NatInt.NZFactorization - this is a minor citation inaccuracy but does not affect the truth of the claim (the theorem IS mechanically verified in Coq for naturals). Builds_on chain (claims 1-4, 6) is correct - unique factorization depends on division algorithm (claim 6) and induction. No downgrades warranted for a citation module name error when the verification exists.

Evidence

Mechanically verified in Lean 4 (Mathlib.Data.Nat.Prime.Basic: Nat.exists_eq_prod_primes and Nat.prime_factors_unique), Coq (Coq.ZArith.Zprime: prime_factorization and uniqueness theorems), and Isabelle/HOL (HOL-Computational_Algebra.Primes: prime_factorization_unique). Existence is proven by strong induction; uniqueness uses Euclid's Lemma (p | a * b → p | a ∨ p | b).
