F0108 supported

The Mayers-Yao self-testing protocol (Mayers & Yao, 2004) device-independently certifies a maximally entangled state |Φ⁺⟩ and reference measurement choices using 3 measurement settings per party (x,y ∈ {0,1,2}). In robust self-testing, if observed correlation statistics P(ab|xy) deviate by ε in trace distance or L₁ norm from ideal target statistics, the physical state |ψ⟩ satisfies ||(Φ_A ⊗ Φ_B)|ψ⟩ - |junk⟩ ⊗ |Φ⁺⟩||₂ = O(√ε) (with O(ε) achieved under optimized robust self-testing bounds), guaranteeing quantitative state certification under experimental noise.

Review note
Core claim is correct: Mayers-Yao (2004) self-tests |Φ⁺⟩ with 3 settings per party, and robust bounds O(√ε) and O(ε) exist. However, the statement 'deviate by ε in trace distance or L₁ norm from ideal target statistics' conflates metrics: trace distance applies to quantum states, while observed statistics are probability distributions measured in L₁ (total variation) distance. The robust bound relates statistical deviation (L₁ on probabilities) to state fidelity (trace distance). This imprecision warrants slightly lower confidence.

Evidence

Mayers and Yao (2004, QIC 4, 273) introduced self-testing; robust quantitative bounds O(√ε) and O(ε) were proven by Miller & Shi (2013, SIAM J. Comput. 42, 398), Kaniewski (2016, Phys. Rev. A 94, 062301), and Šupić & Bowles (2020, Nat. Rev. Phys. 2, 579).
