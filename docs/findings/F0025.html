Review note
The posterior probability calculation for 5-sigma (alpha = 3e-7) yields >0.99999 across realistic priors, but empirical considerations (such as look-elsewhere effects, unquantified systematic errors, and publication bias as illustrated by historical high-sigma signal disappearances) alongside Constitution Rule 3 and F0006 appropriately justify capping base confidence at 0.90–0.95 with a hard maximum cap of 0.99.

Evidence

Bayes' theorem: for α=3e-7, prior=0.05, power=0.80: P(H1|sig) = 0.999993. However, particle physics experience (e.g., 750 GeV diphoton excess at 3.9-sigma that vanished) shows unquantified systematics and look-elsewhere effects dominate at this level. Constitution Rule 3 ('Honesty about uncertainty: Never present a guess as knowledge') and F0006 ('inductive underdetermination and finite sampling') require capping empirical confidence below 1.0. F0010 incorrectly attributed this to Rule 6 (independent review).
