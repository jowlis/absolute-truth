F0078 supported

Domain boundaries and validity conditions for the Mandelstam-Tamm speed limit require: (1) A Hilbert space \mathcal{H} of arbitrary dimension (finite or infinite); (2) A time-independent Hermitian Hamiltonian operator H bounded from below with a well-defined finite energy dispersion \Delta E < \infty; (3) Pure or mixed states \rho(t) undergoing unitary evolution via the von Neumann / Schrödinger equation \partial_t \rho = -\frac{i}{\hbar}[H, \rho]. For systems with infinite energy dispersion (\Delta E \to \infty), the Mandelstam-Tamm bound becomes trivial (\tau \geq 0), necessitating complementary bounds such as Margolus-Levitin.

Review note
Domain boundaries are precisely specified: arbitrary Hilbert space dimension, time-independent Hermitian Hamiltonian bounded from below with finite ΔE, pure/mixed states under unitary evolution. The observation that infinite ΔE makes the bound trivial (τ ≥ 0) is mathematically correct and motivates the need for the Margolus-Levitin complement. Citations to Deffner & Lutz (2017) and Uhlmann (1976) are appropriate. Confidence 0.96 is justified.

Evidence

Established via functional analysis of self-adjoint operators on Hilbert spaces. When \Delta E is undefined or infinite (e.g., for certain position/momentum operator domains without smooth wavepacket support), the denominator diverges, making the bound mathematically vacuous. This necessitates domain restrictions to states in the domain of H^2.

Sources

  • S. Deffner and S. Lutz, 'Quantum Speed Limits: The First Sixty Years', J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 50, 335302 (2017).
  • A. Uhlmann, 'The 'transition probability' in the state space of a *'-algebra', Rep. Math. Phys. 9, 273 (1976).
