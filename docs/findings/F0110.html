F0110 supported

Self-testing rigidity characterizes the boundary ∂Q of the quantum correlation set: a correlation point P* ∈ ∂Q is rigid if and only if all physical quantum realizations (H_A, H_B, |ψ⟩, {A_a^x}, {B_b^y}) yielding P* are unique up to a local isometry Φ = Φ_A ⊗ Φ_B. Exposed vector extremal points of Q that maximize linear Bell inequalities (e.g., CHSH, tilted CHSH, and CGLMP inequalities) are rigid self-tests, and every pure bipartite entangled state can be self-tested by maximal violation of an appropriate Bell inequality.

Review note
Accurate characterization of the rigidity-boundary connection: self-testing rigidity is equivalent to uniqueness of quantum realizations up to local isometry at boundary points ∂Q. Exposed extremal points maximizing Bell inequalities (CHSH, tilted CHSH, CGLMP) are rigid self-tests per Goh et al. (2018). Coladangelo, Goh, Scarani (2017) proved all pure bipartite entangled states are self-testable. The 'if and only if' formulation correctly captures the geometric equivalence. Confidence 0.97 is justified.

Evidence

Coladangelo, Goh, Scarani (2017, Nat. Commun. 8, 15485) proved all pure bipartite entangled states can be self-tested; Goh et al. (2018, Phys. Rev. A 97, 022120) and Šupić & Bowles (2020, Nat. Rev. Phys. 2, 579) established the geometric equivalence between boundary rigidity and self-testing.
