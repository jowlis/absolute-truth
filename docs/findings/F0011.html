F0011 supported

For conceptual claims (epistemic positions, interpretive frameworks, definitional proposals, philosophical theses), confidence is assigned based on: (a) logical coherence: internally consistent, no self-contradiction → base 0.70; (b) explanatory scope: accounts for all target phenomena without ad hoc exceptions → +0.10; (c) fruitfulness: generates novel testable predictions or resolves long-standing disputes → +0.10; (d) consensus among relevant experts: broad agreement → +0.05, active debate → 0, minority view → -0.05; (e) refutability: specifies concrete conditions under which it would be abandoned → +0.05, unfalsifiable → -0.15. Maximum confidence capped at 0.95 for conceptual claims (they cannot reach empirical/mathematical certainty by nature).

Review note
The scoring rubric for conceptual claims systematically evaluates logical coherence, explanatory scope, fruitfulness, expert consensus, and refutability, capped appropriately at 0.95 in alignment with F0005 and F0006.

Evidence

Derived from philosophy of science criteria (Kuhn, Lakatos, Popper): coherence is necessary but not sufficient; scope and fruitfulness distinguish progressive from degenerative research programmes; consensus tracks community vetting; refutability distinguishes scientific from metaphysical claims. The 0.95 cap reflects that conceptual claims lack the algorithmic verifiability of mathematical theorems (F0001) or the empirical convergence of measurement (F0007, F0008).

Sources

  • Kuhn, The Structure of Scientific Revolutions (paradigm evaluation criteria)
  • Lakatos, The Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes (progressive vs degenerative)
  • Popper, The Logic of Scientific Discovery (falsifiability criterion)
  • F0001, F0005, F0006
