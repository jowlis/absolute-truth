Under realistic scientific priors (P(H1) = 0.05-0.15) and statistical power (0.3-0.8), a 2-sigma result (p ≈ 0.05) corresponds to a posterior probability of the alternative hypothesis of only 0.24-0.74, with a typical scenario (prior=0.10, power=0.50) yielding P(H1|sig) ≈ 0.53. This mathematically justifies a base confidence of 0.50-0.55 for 2-sigma empirical claims, not 0.70 as in F0010.

Review note
The Bayesian false discovery rate calculation is mathematically accurate: for alpha = 0.05, prior = 0.10, and power = 0.50, P(H1|sig) = (0.50 * 0.10) / (0.50 * 0.10 + 0.05 * 0.90) = 0.05 / 0.095 ≈ 0.526. The full range of 0.24 to 0.74 across typical priors (0.05–0.15) and powers (0.30–0.80) is correctly computed and provides solid mathematical justification for setting 2-sigma base confidence at 0.50–0.55.

Evidence

Bayes' theorem calculation: FDR = α(1-prior) / [α(1-prior) + power*prior]. For α=0.05, prior=0.10, power=0.50: FDR = 0.474, P(H1|sig) = 0.526. For prior=0.05, power=0.30: FDR = 0.760, P(H1|sig) = 0.240. Calculated via run_python tool. Consistent with Ioannidis (2005) 'Why Most Published Research Findings Are False' and replication crisis literature showing low reproducibility of p<0.05 results.
