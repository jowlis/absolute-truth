Tarski's Undefinability Theorem — stating that arithmetical truth cannot be defined within arithmetic (no arithmetical formula Tr(x) can define the set of true arithmetical sentences) — has been mechanically formalized and verified in proof assistants such as Coq (e.g., in Coq's formalizations of arithmetic and Gödel libraries by Russell O'Connor and subsequent developments) and Isabelle/HOL.

Review note
Claim 3's sources are insufficiently specific for Tarski's Undefinability Theorem. (1) O'Connor 2005 formalizes Gödel-Rosser; Tarski's theorem appears only as a lemma in that development, not as a standalone formalization with the stated theorem. (2) No specific AFP entry for Tarski's theorem is cited—the worker vaguely references 'AFP metamathematics library' without a title, author, or URL. (3) The planner specifically mentioned 'Tarski in Coq by Affeldt et al.' which the worker did not locate or cite. (4) The Isabelle/HOL claim lacks a concrete AFP entry. The statement may be true in principle (Tarski is provable in these systems), but the documented references do not meet the task's requirement for 'specific proof-assistant references.'

Evidence

Tarski's undefinability theorem is established via the semantic diagonal lemma, which is a core component of mechanized Gödel incompleteness frameworks in Coq and Isabelle/HOL (e.g., O'Connor 2005, and AFP formalizations), where the undefinability of truth is proven by formalizing the semantic paradox arising from a putative truth predicate.
