F0093 superseded

Applications to Quantum Cryptography and Quantum Steering: The entropic uncertainty relation with quantum memory serves as a foundational tool for proving the security of Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) protocols (such as BB84 and six-state protocols) against general collective and coherent attacks, without requiring the BB84 source to be characterized or trusted by the receiver (device-independent and source-device-independent frameworks). Furthermore, violations of entropic uncertainty bounds with quantum side information provide quantitative witnesses for entanglement and quantum steering (demonstrating that an observer's reduction in uncertainty can only be achieved if system A is entangled with quantum memory B).

Review note
The application of EUR-QM to QKD security (Tomamichel & Renner 2011) and entanglement/steering witnesses is correctly stated in essence. However, the phrasing 'such as BB84 and six-state protocols... without requiring the BB84 source to be characterized or trusted by the receiver (device-independent and source-device-independent frameworks)' conflates standard BB84 (which assumes a trusted, characterized source) with device-independent/source-device-independent variants that use EUR-QM but are distinct protocols. This imprecision makes the claim overstated as written.

Evidence

Demonstrated in Berta et al. (2010), Tomamichel and Renner (PRL 2011), and reviewed in Coles et al. (Rev. Mod. Phys. 2017), showing direct application to bounding an eavesdropper's guessing probability / conditional entropy in QKD secret key rate formulas.

Sources

  • M. Berta et al., Nature Physics 6, 659 (2010); arXiv:0909.0950
  • M. Tomamichel and R. Renner, Phys. Rev. Lett. 106, 110506 (2011)
