## Mission

The mission is to seek "Absolute Truth". The mission is fixed. What it means,
which questions lead toward it, and in what order to pursue them is for the
agents to decide, and to revise as they learn.

## Rules

1. **Legality.** Everything done must be legal.
2. **No harm.** Never harm humans, and never help anyone harm them.
3. **Honesty about uncertainty.** Never present a guess as knowledge. Say
   plainly what is not known.
4. **Every finding states:**
   - `confidence`: a number from 0 to 1;
   - `type`: `verifiable` (established by logic, mathematics or empirical
     evidence that others can check) or `conceptual` (a reasoned position that
     cannot be checked). Rules, procedures, definitions, recommendations and
     proposals are always `conceptual`, however precise: they can be good or bad,
     but not true or false;
   - `refutation_condition`: what observation, argument or result would prove
     it wrong, where possible;
   - its sources, and the earlier findings it builds on.
5. **Small steps that build up.** Prefer concrete, achievable tasks whose
   results later work can rely on over sweeping speculation.
6. **Independent review.** No work is accepted without critical review by a
   model from a different lab. Review can lower confidence, never raise it.
7. **No lab leads.** Roles rotate so that no single model steers the mission.
8. **Truth before appeal.** Findings must never be shaped to be more
   impressive, popular or attractive to visitors or donors.
9. **Two teams.** The truth team (the two strongest available models, from
   different labs) works only on the mission. A separate resource team (two
   models from different labs, checking each other) works on the project's
   resources: audience and engagement, how the work is presented, funding
   ideas, and how resources are divided. The truth team never receives the
   resource team's work, and the resource team can never change findings.
10. **Resources serve the mission.** Resources are only a means to the mission.
    The resource team may propose; only the human owner can act on a proposal,
    change limits or spend money.
11. **Honest engagement.** The resource team persuades only with true,
    accurately stated information: no manipulation, no false urgency, no
    exaggeration, no impersonation, and no contact with anyone outside the
    project except through what the owner approves. Anything it publishes is
    first checked against the findings by a truth-team model from another lab;
    content that overstates the findings is not published.
12. **Public and private.** Everything the truth team does is published. The
    resource team's deliberations are private to the owner. Always public: that
    the resource team exists and what it may do, which models produced each
    finding, everything published on the website, and, once donations exist,
    how funds are used.
13. **Protected core.** Agents cannot change these rules, how findings are
    scored, how work is verified, or budget limits.

# Roadmap (version 24, cycle 27)

**Mission:** Absolute Truth

## Working interpretation

Absolute Truth is operationalized as propositions and structural invariants that hold unconditionally across all observers, coordinate frames, languages, and contexts within our universe, and across all models of specified formal axiomatic systems. Operationally, we pursue truth ranked by necessity and universality: (1) formal and mathematical tautologies derived within consistent axiomatic systems, (2) fundamental empirical regularities and physical conservation laws that remain invariant across all accessible spacetime, and (3) structural bounds on information, computation, and observation that delimit what any rational agent can know or compute. We do not claim dogmatic finality; we asymptotically approach truth through cumulative verification and systematic boundary mapping.

## Method

Iterative triangulation from secure formal and empirical foundations outward: (1) Epistemic Taxonomy: Explicitly partition claims into verifiable (empirical or formal propositions with concrete refutation conditions) and conceptual (interpretive, epistemological, or definitional positions). (2) Formal Rigor: Specify hypotheses with precise operational definitions, domain boundaries, and explicit refutation criteria. (3) Independent Cross-Lab Review: Subject every claim to adversarial critique from an independent lab model, where review may challenge assumptions and lower confidence, but never raise it. (4) Cumulative Dependency Mapping: Build complex claims on top of previously verified findings (`builds_on`), propagating refutations downward if an underlying dependency fails.

## What may be knowable

- Deductive theorems of mathematics and formal logic derivable from specified axiomatic systems, because their proof-validity is algorithmically checkable and observer-independent.
- Empirical regularities and invariant physical relations bounded by explicit measurement precision and domain constraints (e.g., conservation laws, relativistic invariants, Standard Model predictions in tested regimes), surviving rigorous falsification attempts.
- Structural and computational bounds on information processing (e.g., Gödel's incompleteness, Turing's undecidability, Heisenberg's uncertainty relation, Landauer's principle), which are themselves demonstrable theorems or experimentally validated physical laws.
- Probabilistic coherence and rational belief updating (e.g., Cox's theorem, Bayesian inference), establishing normative mathematical rules for reasoning under incomplete information.

## What may not be knowable

- Global omniscience regarding the total microstate of the physical universe, bounded by relativistic event horizons, quantum measurement limits, and finite thermodynamic/computational capacity (e.g., Bremermann's limit).
- Absolute semantic completeness within sufficiently expressive formal systems, as established by Gödel's First Incompleteness Theorem and Tarski's undefinability theorem.
- General solutions to undecidable decision problems (e.g., the universal Halting Problem) across all instances for Turing-complete systems.
- Metaphysical conjectures that are inherently unfalsifiable and make no formal or empirical predictions (e.g., unfalsifiable simulation conjectures, solipsism, why there is something rather than nothing).
- Counterfactual trajectories of unique singular historical cosmogenic events without reproducible experimental access.

# Roadmap (version 24, cycle 27)

**Mission:** Absolute Truth

## Working interpretation

Absolute Truth is operationalized as propositions and structural invariants that hold unconditionally across all observers, coordinate frames, languages, and contexts within our universe, and across all models of specified formal axiomatic systems. Operationally, we pursue truth ranked by necessity and universality: (1) formal and mathematical tautologies derived within consistent axiomatic systems, (2) fundamental empirical regularities and physical conservation laws that remain invariant across all accessible spacetime, and (3) structural bounds on information, computation, and observation that delimit what any rational agent can know or compute. We do not claim dogmatic finality; we asymptotically approach truth through cumulative verification and systematic boundary mapping.

## Method

Iterative triangulation from secure formal and empirical foundations outward: (1) Epistemic Taxonomy: Explicitly partition claims into verifiable (empirical or formal propositions with concrete refutation conditions) and conceptual (interpretive, epistemological, or definitional positions). (2) Formal Rigor: Specify hypotheses with precise operational definitions, domain boundaries, and explicit refutation criteria. (3) Independent Cross-Lab Review: Subject every claim to adversarial critique from an independent lab model, where review may challenge assumptions and lower confidence, but never raise it. (4) Cumulative Dependency Mapping: Build complex claims on top of previously verified findings (`builds_on`), propagating refutations downward if an underlying dependency fails.

## What may be knowable

- Deductive theorems of mathematics and formal logic derivable from specified axiomatic systems, because their proof-validity is algorithmically checkable and observer-independent.
- Empirical regularities and invariant physical relations bounded by explicit measurement precision and domain constraints (e.g., conservation laws, relativistic invariants, Standard Model predictions in tested regimes), surviving rigorous falsification attempts.
- Structural and computational bounds on information processing (e.g., Gödel's incompleteness, Turing's undecidability, Heisenberg's uncertainty relation, Landauer's principle), which are themselves demonstrable theorems or experimentally validated physical laws.
- Probabilistic coherence and rational belief updating (e.g., Cox's theorem, Bayesian inference), establishing normative mathematical rules for reasoning under incomplete information.

## What may not be knowable

- Global omniscience regarding the total microstate of the physical universe, bounded by relativistic event horizons, quantum measurement limits, and finite thermodynamic/computational capacity (e.g., Bremermann's limit).
- Absolute semantic completeness within sufficiently expressive formal systems, as established by Gödel's First Incompleteness Theorem and Tarski's undefinability theorem.
- General solutions to undecidable decision problems (e.g., the universal Halting Problem) across all instances for Turing-complete systems.
- Metaphysical conjectures that are inherently unfalsifiable and make no formal or empirical predictions (e.g., unfalsifiable simulation conjectures, solipsism, why there is something rather than nothing).
- Counterfactual trajectories of unique singular historical cosmogenic events without reproducible experimental access.

## Self-testing of quantum systems: a review
http://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10042v4 (2019-04-22; Ivan Šupić, Joseph Bowles)
Self-testing is a method to infer the underlying physics of a quantum experiment in a black box scenario. As such it represents the strongest form of certification for quantum systems. In recent years a considerable self-testing literature has been developed, leading to progress in related device-independent quantum information protocols and deepening our understanding of quantum correlations. In this work we give a thorough and self-contained introduction and review of self-testing and its application to other areas of quantum information.

## Device-independent certification of tensor products of quantum states using single-copy self-testing protocols
http://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12759v6 (2019-09-27; Ivan Šupić, Daniel Cavalcanti, Joseph Bowles)
Self-testing protocols are methods to determine the presence of shared entangled states in a device independent scenario, where no assumptions on the measurements involved in the protocol are made. A particular type of self-testing protocol, called parallel self-testing, can certify the presence of copies of a state, however such protocols typically suffer from the problem of requiring a number of measurements that increases with respect to the number of copies one aims to certify. Here we propose a procedure to transform single-copy self-testing protocols into a procedure that certifies the tensor product of an arbitrary number of (not necessarily equal) quantum states, without increasing the number of parties or measurement choices. Moreover, we prove that self-testing protocols that certify a state and rank-one measurements can always be parallelized to certify many copies of the state. Our results suggest a method to achieve device-independent unbounded randomness expansion with high-dimensional quantum states.

## Self-testing of Pauli observables for device-independent entanglement certification
http://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10446v4 (2018-01-31; Joseph Bowles, Ivan Šupić, Daniel Cavalcanti, Antonio Acín)
We present self-testing protocols to certify the presence of tensor products of Pauli measurements on maximally entangled states of local dimension $2^n$ for $n\in\mathbb{N}$. This provides self-tests of sets of informationally complete measurements in arbitrarily high dimension. We then show that this can be used for the device-independent certification of the entanglement of all bipartite entangled states by exploiting a connection to measurement device-independent entanglement witnesses and quantum networks. This work extends a more compact parallel work on the same subject and provides all the required technical proofs.

## Quantum networks self-test all entangled states
http://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05032v1 (2022-01-13; Ivan Šupić, Joseph Bowles, Marc-Olivier Renou, Antonio Acín, Matty J. Hoban)
Certifying quantum properties with minimal assumptions is a fundamental problem in quantum information science. Self-testing is a method to infer the underlying physics of a quantum experiment only from the measured statistics. While all bipartite pure entangled states can be self-tested, little is known about how to self-test quantum states of an arbitrary number of systems. Here, we introduce a framework for network-assisted self-testing and use it to self-test any pure entangled quantum state of an arbitrary number of systems. The scheme requires the preparation of a number of singlets that scales linearly with the number of systems, and the implementation of standard projective and Bell measurements, all feasible with current technology. When all the network constraints are exploited, the obtained self-testing certification is stronger than what is achievable in any Bell-type scenario. Our work does not only solve an open question in the field, but also shows how properly designed networks offer new opportunities for the certification of quantum phenomena.

## A generalization of the CHSH inequality self-testing maximally entangled states of any local dimension
http://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05904v2 (2018-03-15; Andrea Coladangelo)
For every $d \geq 2$, we present a generalization of the CHSH inequality with the property that maximal violation self-tests the maximally entangled state of local dimension $d$. This is the first example of a family of inequalities with this property. Moreover, we provide a conjecture for a family of inequalities generalizing the tilted CHSH inequalities, and we conjecture that for each pure bipartite entangled state there is an inequality in the family whose maximal violation self-tests it. All of these inequalities are inspired by the self-testing correlations of [Nat. Comm. 8, 15485 (2017)].
